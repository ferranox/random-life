Random Life Generator

Would you swap your life for a random life? Press below to generate a statistically plausible fictional person from anywhere on Earth. This free random person generator builds a complete fictional identity — country, age, occupation, income and more — with probabilities matched to real global data.


What each random life includes

Every life generated by this fictional person generator is an original, statistically weighted identity — not a copy of a real individual:

  • Random country and nationality — picked from all World Bank members (plus Taiwan), weighted by live population.
  • Age and sex — drawn from each country's age structure and female population share.
  • Urban or rural home — decided by the national urbanisation rate.
  • Occupation — a random occupation generator refined by income group, habitat, employment mix and unemployment rate.
  • Income estimate — a rough annual figure in nominal USD based on GDP per capita, occupation and age.
  • Life expectancy, utilities and family — life expectancy at birth, access to electricity, drinking water, sanitation and the internet, plus number of children.

How the random life generator works

  1. Pick a country at random, weighted by population — bigger countries appear more often.
  2. Draw demographics — sex, age band, and urban vs. rural habitat from national rates.
  3. Assign work and income — sample an occupation from income-group and sector data, then estimate income.
  4. Fill in daily life — life expectancy, electricity, water, sanitation, internet access and children.

Live World Bank indicators refresh in your browser every 12 hours, with a built-in fallback dataset when offline. For full sources and per-indicator details, open Data & methodology in the footer below.

Random life generator FAQ

What is a random life generator?

A random life generator creates an entirely fictional person — their country, age, sex, urban or rural home, occupation, income and more. Random Life weights every draw by real global statistics, so results stay statistically plausible.

Are the generated people real?

No. Every random person is entirely fictional. Names are illustrative and grouped by broad cultural region, and no attempt is made to represent any real individual, living or dead.

How does Random Life pick each person?

The country is picked at random weighted by population, then sex, age, urban or rural habitat, occupation, income, life expectancy, access to electricity, water, sanitation and internet, and number of children are drawn from that country's World Bank indicators.

What can I use a random person generator for?

Writing prompts, role-playing and tabletop NPCs, classroom demographics lessons, empathy exercises such as asking what if I was born somewhere else, and exploring global population statistics.

Is Random Life free, and does it work offline?

Yes. Random Life is a free random life generator with no sign-up. It runs as a static site, caches data in your browser, and keeps working offline once loaded.

Where does the data come from?

Country statistics come from World Bank Open Data, age-structure figures rest on the UN World Population Prospects, and occupation categories follow International Labour Organization (ILO) definitions.

      Data Sources & Methodology

      Data sources

      • World Bank Open Data - country list, income group, and region, plus population, life expectancy (male and female), urbanisation rate, GDP per capita, electricity access, basic drinking water, basic sanitation, internet use, fertility rate, age structure (share aged 0-14 and 65+), sex split (female share of population), employment mix (share in agriculture / industry / services), and unemployment rate.
      • UN Population Division - the age-structure figures rest on its World Population Prospects, and it informs the within-income-group age splits.
      • International Labour Organization (ILO) - reference for the employment categories and occupation mix; the per-country sector shares and unemployment rates are its ILO-modelled estimates published via the World Bank.

      Live data

      When online, the app fetches the country list (names, income groups, regions - so new and changed countries appear with no manual update) and the most recent value (mrv=5, newest available kept) for these World Bank indicators, merging them over the built-in fallback dataset:

      • SP.POP.TOTL - total population
      • SP.DYN.LE00.MA.IN - life expectancy at birth, male
      • SP.DYN.LE00.FE.IN - life expectancy at birth, female
      • SP.URB.TOTL.IN.ZS - urban population (% of total)
      • NY.GDP.PCAP.CD - GDP per capita (current US$)
      • EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS - access to electricity (% of population)
      • SH.H2O.BASW.ZS - people using at least basic drinking water services (%)
      • SH.STA.BASS.ZS - people using at least basic sanitation services (%)
      • IT.NET.USER.ZS - individuals using the internet (% of population)
      • SP.DYN.TFRT.IN - fertility rate (births per woman)
      • SP.POP.0014.TO.ZS - population ages 0-14 (% of total)
      • SP.POP.65UP.TO.ZS - population ages 65+ (% of total)
      • SP.POP.TOTL.FE.ZS - population, female (% of total)
      • SL.AGR.EMPL.ZS - employment in agriculture (% of total employment)
      • SL.IND.EMPL.ZS - employment in industry (% of total employment)
      • SL.SRV.EMPL.ZS - employment in services (% of total employment)
      • SL.UEM.TOTL.ZS - unemployment (% of total labour force)

      Flags are derived from country codes and need no data source. Results are cached in your browser (refreshed every 12 hours) so they are not re-fetched on every reload. If the API is unavailable, the app falls back to cached and then built-in data.

      Methodology

      • Country - picked at random from all World Bank members (plus Taiwan), weighted by live population, so bigger countries turn up more often.
      • Sex - drawn from the country's female share of the population.
      • Age - drawn from age bands fitted to the country's own share of children (0-14) and older people (65+), with the gaps filled in from its income-group pattern.
      • Urban vs. rural - decided by the country's urbanisation rate.
      • Occupation - sampled from a list weighted by income group and habitat, refined per country by its employment mix (agriculture / industry / services) and unemployment rate, limited to plausible working ages, with student and retirement shares that differ by income group.
      • Income - estimated from GDP per capita, an occupation multiplier, an age adjustment, plus some random variation. A rough figure in nominal USD, not PPP.
      • Life expectancy - the national average at birth for the person's sex.
      • Electricity, drinking water, sanitation, and internet - the person either has it or not, drawn from the national rate (a country with 63% electricity access gives each person a 63% chance).
      • Children - drawn from the national fertility rate and adjusted for age: under-18s have none, and completed family size is approached by the mid-40s.

      Limitations

      • Income figures are rough estimates in nominal USD - not adjusted for local purchasing power (PPP).
      • Within working-age bands, the age mix follows income-group patterns rather than the country's exact 5-year pyramid.
      • Names are illustrative, grouped by broad cultural region, and are not exhaustive or country-specific.
      • "Current" data reflects the most recently available World Bank figures, which typically lag by 1-2 years.

      Note

      All generated people are entirely fictional. No attempt is made to represent any real individual, living or dead.
