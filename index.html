Data Sources & Methodology

Data sources

  • World Bank Open Data — population, life expectancy (male & female), urbanisation rate, GDP per capita, electricity access, basic drinking water, basic sanitation, internet use, and fertility rate.
  • UN Population Division — informs the age-distribution model used per income group.
  • International Labour Organization (ILO) — reference for the employment categories and occupation mix.

Live data

When online, the app fetches the most recent value (mrv=5, newest available kept) for these World Bank indicators and merges them over the built-in dataset:

  • SP.POP.TOTL — total population
  • SP.DYN.LE00.MA.IN — life expectancy at birth, male
  • SP.DYN.LE00.FE.IN — life expectancy at birth, female
  • SP.URB.TOTL.IN.ZS — urban population (% of total)
  • NY.GDP.PCAP.CD — GDP per capita (current US$)
  • EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS — access to electricity (% of population)
  • SH.H2O.BASW.ZS — people using at least basic drinking water services (%)
  • SH.STA.BASS.ZS — people using at least basic sanitation services (%)
  • IT.NET.USER.ZS — individuals using the internet (% of population)
  • SP.DYN.TFRT.IN — fertility rate (births per woman)

Results are cached in your browser session so they are not re-fetched on every reload. If the API is unavailable, the app falls back to cached and then built-in data.

Methodology

  • Country is chosen at random, weighted by population — so more populous countries appear more often, mirroring where people actually live.
  • Age is drawn from age-band weights that differ by the country's World Bank income group (low, lower-middle, upper-middle, high), reflecting younger populations in lower-income countries.
  • Urban vs. rural is decided by the country's urbanisation rate.
  • Occupation is sampled from an income-group- and habitat-weighted list, constrained by plausible working ages, with student/retirement shares that vary by income group.
  • Income is modelled from GDP per capita, an occupation multiplier, an age adjustment, and log-normal noise. It is an order-of-magnitude estimate, not a precise figure.
  • Life expectancy is the national average for the person's sex.
  • Electricity, drinking water, sanitation, and internet are individualised: the person either has it or not, drawn from the national access rate (e.g. a country with 63% electricity access gives each person a 63% chance). The national rate is shown alongside.
  • Children is the person's individualised number of children, drawn from the national fertility rate and adjusted for age — under-18s have none, and completed family size is approached by the mid-40s. The national average is shown alongside for context.

Limitations

  • Income figures are modelled estimates in nominal USD — not adjusted for local purchasing power (PPP).
  • Age distributions approximate broad income-group patterns rather than each country's exact pyramid.
  • Name lists are illustrative, grouped by broad cultural region, and are not exhaustive or country-specific.
  • "Current" data reflects the most recently available World Bank figures, which typically lag by 1–2 years.

Note

All generated people are entirely fictional. No attempt is made to represent any real individual, living or dead.
