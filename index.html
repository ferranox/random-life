      Data Sources & Methodology

      Data sources

      The numbers behind each generated life come from the official sources below. Reference years are stated because they are not all the same.

      • UN World Population Prospects 2024 (UN DESA, Population Division; medium variant, including the January 2026 interim update for Togo). Reference year 2023 (1 July), the last year the UN labels an estimate rather than a projection. Used for: total population (country weighting), female share, the population at each single year of age 0-100 (both sexes pooled), total fertility rate, and life expectancy at birth for men and women. Built into the site (snapshot); not fetched live.
      • UN World Urbanization Prospects 2025 (UN DESA, Population Division). Share of the population living in urban areas under each country's own national definition of "urban" (not the Degree of Urbanization classification), 2023. Built into the site; not fetched live.
      • ILO modelled estimates, November 2025 (ILOSTAT). Employment by broad sector (agriculture / industry / services, % of employed people aged 15+) and the unemployment rate (% of the labour force aged 15+), 2024, the latest full year in the release. These are modelled estimates: where a country has no recent survey the ILO imputes values. Built into the site; not fetched live.
      • World Bank Open Data (World Development Indicators) (data.worldbank.org). GDP per capita (current US$), access to electricity, basic drinking water, basic sanitation and internet use, plus each country's income group and region. Newest value per country within the last five years. The access and internet series are published by the World Bank from their originators (SDG 7.1.1 electrification dataset; WHO/UNICEF Joint Monitoring Programme; International Telecommunication Union). Fetched live when online, with a built-in snapshot as fallback.

      Where a UN or ILO source has no value for a country, the matching World Bank series is used if it has one; otherwise the value is left blank and the generator uses its default (for example, the field shows N/A).

      Live data

      When online, the app fetches the World Bank country list (income groups and regions) and the most recent value (mrv=5, newest available kept) for these five indicators. Each one is checked (expected structure, finite numbers in a sensible range, known country codes) and applied on its own; anything that fails keeps its built-in value.

      • NY.GDP.PCAP.CD - GDP per capita (current US$)
      • EG.ELC.ACCS.ZS - access to electricity (% of population)
      • SH.H2O.BASW.ZS - people using at least basic drinking water services (%)
      • SH.STA.BASS.ZS - people using at least basic sanitation services (%)
      • IT.NET.USER.ZS - individuals using the internet (% of population)

      The UN and ILO figures are bulk downloads that are revised only every year or two, so they are not requested from your browser: they are built into the site as a compact snapshot, which also means the first life can be generated with no network. Flags are derived from country codes and need no data source. Live results are cached in your browser (refreshed every 12 hours). If the API is unavailable, the app falls back to cached and then built-in data.

      Methodology

      • Country - picked at random from all World Bank members (plus Taiwan), weighted by UN population, so bigger countries turn up more often.
      • Sex - drawn from the country's female share of the population.
      • Age - drawn from the country's UN population at each single year of age (0 to 100; 100 means 100 or older). Age is drawn for both sexes together, so it does not depend on sex. If a country had no age data the older six-band approximation would be used instead; currently only the Channel Islands rely on it.
      • Urban vs. rural - decided by the country's urban share under its national definition.
      • Occupation - Random Life's own model: a hand-built list weighted by income group and habitat, refined per country by its employment mix (agriculture / industry / services) and unemployment rate, limited to plausible working ages, with student and retirement shares that differ by income group.
      • Income - Random Life's own construction: GDP per capita multiplied by hand-built ratios for income group, occupation and age, plus random variation. A rough figure in nominal USD, not PPP.
      • Habitation - Random Life's own dwelling-type tables by income group and urban / rural.
      • Life expectancy - the national average at birth for the person's sex. It is a population average, not a prediction for any individual.
      • Electricity, drinking water, sanitation, and internet - Random Life's own probability model: the person either has it or not, drawn from the national rate (a country with 63% electricity access gives each person a 63% chance). A national percentage is a probability, not a claim about any particular household.
      • Children - drawn from the national total fertility rate and adjusted for age (Random Life's own rule): under-18s have none, and completed family size is approached by the mid-40s.

      Official data and Random Life's own modelling

      • Official data: World Bank GDP per capita, electricity, water, sanitation, internet, income group and region.
      • Official modelled data: UN population, age, fertility, life expectancy and urban figures are UN estimates built from censuses, surveys and demographic models; ILO sector and unemployment figures are ILO modelled estimates.
      • Random Life's own assumptions (not official statistics): occupation probabilities, the income construction, dwelling-type tables, student and retired shares, the probability model for water, sanitation, electricity and internet, and the age rules for children. Official inputs do not make these outputs official or accurate for any real person.

      Limitations

      • Income figures are rough estimates in nominal USD - not adjusted for local purchasing power (PPP).
      • Population, age, fertility, life expectancy and urban figures refer to 2023, sector and unemployment figures to 2024, and World Bank values to the newest year available (typically 1-2 years old). Age is not drawn conditional on sex.
      • Names are illustrative, grouped by broad cultural region, and are not exhaustive or country-specific.
      • Some small territories lack values in some sources and show N/A. Taiwan is not covered by the World Bank, so its GDP per capita and access figures are earlier approximations that have no official source here.
